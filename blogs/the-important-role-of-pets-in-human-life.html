The Important Role of Pets in Human Life

Jordan Vale · Wellness writer & shelter volunteer · Updated 2026

They're not just pets. Across centuries and cultures, animals have walked beside us as friends, workers, healers and teachers. Here's why their role in our lives is bigger — and deeper — than we often give them credit for.

In this guide
  1. A faithful friend
  2. Bodies that move together
  3. Minds that feel less alone
  4. Part of the family
  5. Richer, fuller lives

A faithful friend

At the heart of it all is friendship — the kind that doesn't judge your messy day or your quiet moods. Animals offer unconditional presence, and that's rarer than it sounds.

"The best therapist doesn't charge by the hour and always greets you at the door."

Bodies that move together

Dogs especially get us moving. That daily walk isn't just for them — it's a nudge toward sunlight, fresh air and a healthier heart for you. The companionship and the exercise arrive together.

Minds that feel less alone

Pets are natural conversation-starters — at the park, the vet, the dog class. They quietly weave us into social fabrics, which is powerful medicine for loneliness. For many, a pet is the reason to get out of bed, and that matters enormously.

Part of the family

We celebrate their birthdays, save them a seat, and talk to them like old friends. Pets are woven into the rhythm of family life — witness to our best and worst days, and often the glue that holds a household together.

~63%of homes own a pet

Richer, fuller lives

From toddlers learning empathy to elders finding purpose, pets shape us at every stage. They teach responsibility, patience, loss and love — lessons that make us better humans.

Think about it: almost every meaningful human skill — gentleness, routine, unconditional love — is practiced, daily, with a companion.
